Best TVs Under $1,000 (2026)

Updated July 2026  ·  Ranked on published independent measurements, not price.

The Short Version

  • Best overall & brightest: The Hisense U8QG — RTINGS calls it the best Hisense they've tested and measured HDR peak brightness in the ~3,900–4,000+ nit range. Under $1,000 applies to the 55-inch.
  • Best value mini-LED: The TCL QM7K (65") — roughly 2,800 dimming zones and ~2,400+ measured nits for a lot less money.
  • Best smart platform / gaming hub: The Samsung QN70F Neo QLED — Tizen, Samsung Gaming Hub, ~2,000-nit class. (No Dolby Vision.)
  • Best OLED under $1,000: The LG C-series OLED (42") — perfect blacks and elite gaming, but only at small sizes in this budget.
Grab your pick: Hisense U8QG 55" (~$999)  ·  TCL QM7K 65" (~$800–$999)  ·  Samsung QN70F 55–65" (~$800–$1,000)  ·  TCL QM6K 65" (~$700)  ·  LG C-series OLED 42" (~$900–$1,000)

Here is the honest state of the sub-$1,000 TV market in 2026: mini-LED won it. A few years ago you could squeeze an entry OLED into this band at 55 inches. That has largely stopped. Budget OLED at big sizes has drifted above $1,000, leaving mini-LED — QLED panels with hundreds or thousands of independently dimmable backlight zones — as the dominant value technology. OLED still shows up under a grand, but only at smaller and/or outgoing-generation panels (think 42–48 inch). We'll be blunt about which size each price refers to, because "under $1,000" almost always means a specific size or a sale.

How We Evaluate

Loiter Point does not run a TV lab. We do not own a test bench, a colorimeter, or a darkroom, and we will never pretend otherwise. What we do is synthesize published independent measurements — the RTINGS-style labs that measure peak brightness in nits, contrast, input lag, color volume and dimming behavior under controlled conditions — alongside hands-on impressions from Tom's Guide, TechRadar and Digital Trends, and verified owner reports about reliability and real-room performance.

Every performance figure in this guide is attributed to the outlet that produced it. When a number is a lab measurement we label it "RTINGS-measured"; when it comes from other hands-on testing we say "independent testing reports"; when it is a real-world estimate or an owner observation we mark it as such. Rankings are ordered on that evidence, not on price and not on affiliate payout. Where reviewers disagree, or where the public data is thin, we say so out loud rather than papering over it.

Nerd Box

Mini-LED zones vs OLED per-pixel: why brightness is the whole argument

A mini-LED TV lights its picture with a grid of backlight zones — the QM7K has around 2,800 of them, the QM6K rather fewer. Each zone can brighten or dim on its own, which is how these sets get deep blacks next to searing highlights. But a zone is still bigger than a pixel, so a bright object on a black field can leak light into its surrounding zone. That halo is called blooming, and more zones means less of it.

OLED sidesteps the problem entirely: every pixel makes its own light and can switch fully off, giving perfect blacks and zero blooming. So why isn't OLED the automatic winner under $1,000? Two reasons. First, in this budget OLED only comes in small panels. Second — and this is the single thing mini-LED does that OLED at this price cannot — raw brightness. Nits measure light output, and they matter most for HDR highlights and for fighting daylight in a bright room. RTINGS measured the U8QG near 4,000 nits; an OLED in this band is a fraction of that. If your room has windows, brightness wins. If your room is dark and contrast is king, OLED wins. That single tradeoff decides most of this list.

Bottom Line

There is no single "best" TV under $1,000 — there's the best one for your room and how you use it. Here's the quick read:

Bright room
Hisense U8QG (55"). RTINGS-measured ~4,000 nits beats daylight; nothing else here is close on brightness.
Gaming
LG C-series OLED for a desk or dark room (144Hz, G-Sync, near-zero lag), or the TCL QM7K if you want a big bright living-room gaming screen with FreeSync.
Budget / big screen
TCL QM6K. 65" around $700, 75" sometimes under $1,000 — the most competent panel per dollar.
Movies / dark room
LG C-series OLED. Perfect blacks and per-pixel contrast win where brightness doesn't matter.
Smart platform
Samsung QN70F. Tizen + Gaming Hub and the best app ecosystem — just remember, no Dolby Vision.

If you want one recommendation to stop reading on: the Hisense U8QG at 55 inches is the most complete TV in this band on published evidence. If your budget is tighter or your room is bigger, the TCL QM7K gives up remarkably little for the money. Prices in this category move constantly, so check the current number before you buy.
