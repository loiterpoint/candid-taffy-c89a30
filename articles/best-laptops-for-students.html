Buying a student laptop in 2026 is a strange exercise, and it is worth naming why up front: the year has been dominated by a global memory shortage. DRAM and, to a lesser extent, SSD prices have surged, and that surge has rippled straight into the price of finished laptops. Apple raised MacBook Air prices by $200 in June, retailers have quietly nudged sticker prices up, and stock on popular configurations has been thin. This is not a normal shopping season where you wait for a predictable back-to-school sale and pounce.

So this guide does two things differently. First, it ranks by evidence and fit, not by what pays us the most — the order below reflects published independent testing and owner reports, and it would be the same order if there were no affiliate links at all. Second, it is deliberately conservative about numbers. We quote Apple's own prices (because Apple publishes them) with a volatility caveat, and for everything else we point you at live retailer listings rather than pretend we know today's price to the dollar. When prices move week to week, a number frozen into an article is worse than no number.

What matters for a student laptop has not changed: all-day battery so you are not hunting for outlets between lectures, weight low enough that you will carry it, 16GB of RAM as a sensible baseline so the machine lasts several years, a durable build that survives a backpack, and USB-C charging so you can share a cable with a phone or a friend. If you already have a desk setup in mind, our guides to USB-C hubs and portable monitors pair well with any pick here. And if a laptop is not really what you need, our tablet guide is worth a look.

How We Evaluate Student Laptops

Loiter Point does not run a hardware lab. We say that plainly because a lot of "best laptop" content implies bench time that never happened. Instead, we synthesize three things: published independent testing (primarily RTINGS, which buys and tests laptops itself — around 175 models across their database), owner reports and long-term reviews, and manufacturer spec sheets. Then we cross-check that against current retail listings.

Because of that, we tag specs plainly. A rated figure comes from a manufacturer's spec sheet — a claim, not a verdict. A est. real-world (reported) figure is derived from independent reviews or owner reports. We do not use the word "measured" anywhere, because we did not measure anything. Where we lack a number we can source, we leave the cell blank and say so, rather than inventing a battery-hours figure or a brightness spec to fill space. That is why the non-Apple cards below carry fewer hard numbers than you might expect: those are the real limits of what we can attribute.

Ranking is by evidence and fit for a student, full stop. Affiliate relationships never move a pick up or down the list.

Why laptop prices are strange in 2026

The 2026 memory shortage is the single biggest reason this guide reads the way it does. DRAM (the memory chips that become a laptop's RAM) and, to a lesser degree, NAND (the SSD storage) have been in short supply, and prices for those components have surged. A laptop is partly a bundle of those chips, so when the chips get expensive, the finished laptop gets expensive too.

That has an uncomfortable implication for shopping: the more RAM and storage a configuration has, the more of the scarce component it contains, and the more its price inflates. It also explains why budget models get hit hardest. Cheap laptops run on thin margins, so there is little slack to absorb a component-cost jump — the increase gets passed to you more directly than it does on a premium machine.

Apple is the clearest example we can point to with a source. Per Apple and reporting from MacRumors, Apple raised MacBook Air prices by $200 in June 2026, tying the move to the memory situation; a separate MacRumors report on August 2 noted supply shortages with 2–6 week delivery estimates. That is exactly why we quote "recently seen — check listing" for the non-Apple picks instead of hard numbers. A price we freeze into this page today could be wrong by next week, and a stale number is worse than an honest "go check."

Chromebook vs Windows vs Mac for a student

Pick the operating system your coursework requires, not the brand with the nicest ad. Start with the software list. Some programs mandate Windows-only applications (certain engineering, statistics, or accounting suites); if yours does, a Chromebook or a Mac can become a daily headache no amount of battery life fixes. Ask your department or check a syllabus before you spend a cent.

ChromeOS (the Acer Spin 714) is ideal if your day is a browser, Google Workspace, and video calls. It is fast, cheap, and low-maintenance — but offline app support is limited and heavy local software is off the table. Windows (the Zenbook A14, Vivobook 16, Yoga 7) runs the widest range of specialized software and gives you the most choice at every price. macOS (the MacBook Air M5) is the smoothest all-rounder with the best battery and resale in this guide, as long as your required apps run on it.

If you are torn between a laptop and a tablet-plus-keyboard setup for a browser-and-notes lifestyle, our tablet guide lays out that trade-off. When in doubt, buy the OS that runs your must-have software, then optimize for battery and weight within that lane.

Bottom Line

If your budget stretches, make the MacBook Air 13 (M5) the default. It is RTINGS' top college pick line for good reason — the battery, build quality, and resale value make it the machine most students will be happiest with over three or four years. Buy it through Apple's education store if you qualify, budget for possible shipping delays, and accept that the June price hike is the cost of buying in a shortage year. Only step up to the 15" if you specifically want the extra screen space.

If the budget does not stretch, the ASUS Vivobook 16 is the value play — RTINGS' budget-student pick, with a big screen and a build sturdier than its plastic implies. Watch the listing, because budget models are the most exposed to 2026 price swings. And if your life happens inside a browser, the Acer Chromebook Plus Spin 714 is the cheapest properly good option, as long as your required software runs on ChromeOS.

Whatever you choose, remember the timing caveat. This is a volatile year: prices are up, stock is thin, and the number you see today may not be the number tomorrow.
