Best QLED TVs (2026)

Every TV here is a quantum-dot LCD (QLED), and four of the five pair those quantum dots with a mini-LED backlight for deeper contrast and higher brightness. We ranked them strictly on merit — picture, brightness, sound and value as reported by independent testing and owners — not on price. If you want the short version, it's right below.

Nerd-box: QLED vs mini-LED vs OLED, and why "5,000 nits" isn't 5,000 nits

QLED is an LCD TV with a quantum-dot layer for richer, more saturated color. It still needs a backlight behind the LCD. Mini-LED upgrades that backlight to thousands of tiny LEDs grouped into dimming zones — independently controlled patches that dim or brighten to deepen black levels and boost highlights. More zones means finer control and less "blooming" (that faint halo around bright objects on a dark background). OLED is a different technology entirely: every pixel makes its own light, so blacks are perfect and there are no zones — but OLED generally can't hit the raw brightness these mini-LEDs can, which is why QLED/mini-LED wins in bright rooms.

About "nits." A nit is a unit of brightness. The catch: a rated peak figure is usually a best-case, momentary flash on a tiny window (say 2–10% of the screen). Real content rarely lights up just a sliver, so full-screen brightness is far lower — the U8QG's ~3,384-nit 10%-window measurement drops to ~800 nits full-screen. That's why we split every spec grid into rated (the manufacturer's claim) and est. real-world / reported (what independent reviewers actually measured). When you see "up to 5,000 nits," read it as a ceiling, not a promise.

#4

Hisense U7 (65U75QG)

Best mid-range

Street ~$1,000–1,300 for 65"

The U7 is the sensible step down from the U8QG: still a native-165Hz mini-LED with VRR up to 288 and up to ~3,000 dimming zones, which makes it a strong gaming and sports set. Hisense rates it up to 3,000 nits, but expect real-world peak to land below that — on mid-tier mini-LEDs, reviewers commonly report roughly half to two-thirds of the rated figure, and independent measurements vary, so we won't quote a single number. Against its bigger sibling it's a clear step down in both peak brightness and sound (2.1.2 vs 4.1.2 channels). If the U8QG is out of budget, this is the one.

How We Evaluate QLED TVs

Loiter Point does not run a test lab. We don't own light meters, we don't put units on a bench, and we'd never claim a nit reading as our own. What we do instead is synthesize the best published independent testing with verified owner reports, then rank on the balance of that evidence.

For this guide that means cross-referencing measured brightness, contrast, blooming, input lag and sound quality from RTINGS (best mini-LED and best budget picks), Tom's Guide (QN90F and U8QG reviews, best-Samsung and best-budget roundups), Sound & Vision (65U8QG review), AVSForum (U8QG review) and TechRadar (QM8), alongside patterns in owner feedback. When those sources disagree — or when a rated figure and a measured figure diverge, as they routinely do on peak brightness — we flag it rather than paper over it. Every real-world number here is attributed to that published testing or to owner reports and labeled "est. real-world" or "reported," never presented as our own bench result. Where the evidence is thin, we tell you it's thin.

Bottom Line

Buy the Samsung QN90F if you want the best all-rounder and the best bright-room set, and you value glare control and processing over a bigger nit number. Buy the Hisense U8QG if you want the most measured brightness and the best built-in sound per dollar — especially if you can catch it on sale. Go TCL QM8K for a big 85"+ screen, step down to the Hisense U7 if the U8QG stretches your budget, and grab the TCL QM6K if you want real mini-LED for the least money. Whatever you pick, wait for a sale if you can — on these models it can be the difference of several hundred dollars.
